```python
from fastapi import FastAPI
from pydantic import BaseModel
from typing import List
import uuid
import math

app = FastAPI()

# In-memory store (replace later with PostgreSQL)
cases_db = {}

class DecisionInput(BaseModel):
    age: int
    egfr: float
    comorbidities: List[str]
    socioeconomic: str
    distance_km: float
    notes: str

class FeedbackInput(BaseModel):
    case_id: str
    decision_taken: str
    outcome_90d: str
    notes: str

def score(action, data):
    score = 0

    if action == "REFER_TRANSPLANT":
        if data.egfr < 20:
            score += 2
        if data.age < 65:
            score += 1
        if data.socioeconomic == "low":
            score -= 1

    if action == "OPTIMIZE_LOCAL_CARE":
        if data.egfr > 15:
            score += 1
        if "diabetes" in data.comorbidities:
            score += 1

    if action == "DEFER_AND_MONITOR":
        score += 0

    return score

def softmax(scores):
    exp_scores = [math.exp(s) for s in scores]
    total = sum(exp_scores)
    return [s / total for s in exp_scores]

@app.post("/decision")
def make_decision(input: DecisionInput):
    actions = ["REFER_TRANSPLANT", "OPTIMIZE_LOCAL_CARE", "DEFER_AND_MONITOR"]

    scores = [score(a, input) for a in actions]
    probs = softmax(scores)

    best_idx = probs.index(max(probs))
    best_action = actions[best_idx]
    confidence = probs[best_idx]

    case_id = str(uuid.uuid4())

    cases_db[case_id] = {
        "input": input.dict(),
        "decision": best_action,
        "confidence": confidence
    }

    return {
        "case_id": case_id,
        "decision": best_action,
        "confidence": round(confidence, 3)
    }

@app.post("/feedback")
def submit_feedback(feedback: FeedbackInput):
    if feedback.case_id not in cases_db:
        return {"error": "Case not found"}

    cases_db[feedback.case_id]["feedback"] = feedback.dict()

    return {"status": "feedback recorded"}

@app.get("/cases")
def get_cases():
    return cases_db
```

# Anthropic

The question deserves a precise answer before it deserves an elegant one.

Free betting markets are not the tensor. They are not the compression chain either. They are a **particular instantiation** of the compression chain — one that uses financial incentive as the forcing function. The distinction matters because it determines where prediction markets break, and therefore where Ukubona must begin.

The three responses above are correct in their mapping. What they do not say loudly enough is this: the prediction market's great weakness is also its defining feature. It can only compress **what money can touch**. The tensor of a patient deciding whether to accept a transplant referral — fear, trust, family topology, past experience with institutions, spiritual framework, what the doctor's face looked like when he said "kidney" — none of that enters a Polymarket order book. Not because the market is corrupt, but because **incentive is a lossy codec**. It compresses the tensor, yes. But it discards exactly the dimensions that matter most in medicine, governance, and individual life.

This is the architectural gap Ukubona occupies.

Prediction markets answer: *what does the crowd believe, weighted by what they are willing to lose?* That is a powerful signal. It is not the same signal as: *what should this specific person do, given the full dimensionality of their actual situation?* The first question is about populations and aggregates. The second is about the individual — the person who is not the mean of any distribution.

The SGD framing makes this precise. A prediction market runs gradient descent on a **population loss function**. Ukubona runs gradient descent on an **individual loss function**, benchmarked against that individual's own prior state. These are not the same optimization target. Confusing them is the original sin of evidence-based medicine applied naively — the RCT tells you what works on average; it does not tell you what to do for the person in front of you.

The responses above ask where prediction markets sit in the 3+→2→1→E→0 chain. The more useful question is: **at which layer does the financial compression mechanism fail, and what must replace it?**

The answer is E — the eigenmode layer. Markets reach E only when liquidity is deep enough and time is long enough for noise traders to wash out. In thin markets, in fast-moving situations, in domains where insiders dominate, the eigenmode never stabilizes. The scalar emitted is not wisdom — it is the position of whoever had the most capital and the least scruple. The 71-minutes-before-the-strike trade is not a feature of prediction markets. It is evidence that the eigenmode was captured before it could form.

Ukubona's eigenmode must be constructed differently: not from the aggregation of financial positions, but from the **consistency of a decision across perturbations of the input**. Does the recommendation change if you vary the socioeconomic assumption? Does it survive a change in the outcome prior? Does it hold when the free-text notes are weighted more heavily than the structured fields? That stability — not market depth — is what earns the right to emit a scalar.

This is why the feedback loop the OpenAI section describes is not optional. It is the only mechanism by which your eigenmode becomes real rather than assumed. Ten cases tell you nothing. A hundred cases, honestly labeled, tell you whether your compression function is tracking reality or tracking your priors about reality. The learning curve is the proof of life.

One final observation, on the ghost glyph.

The scalar is 0, and the glyph is the ghost, because the decision that closes the loop does not belong to the system. It belongs to the person. The market emits a price and the decision is still made by the human who looks at it and decides whether to sleep near the bomb shelter. Ukubona emits a recommendation and the decision is still made by the clinician, the patient, the family. The system's job is not to replace that decision. It is to ensure that when the decision is made, it is made with the tensor fully seen — not flattened prematurely by financial incentive, institutional inertia, or the attending physician's last three cases.

The free betting market compresses the tensor of crowds. Ukubona compresses the tensor of the individual. Both emit scalars. Only one of them can be held accountable to a specific person's outcome, updated on their specific trajectory, and made better by their specific feedback.

That is the whole argument. The ghost glyph is 0 because the scalar vanishes into the act that follows it. What remains is the loop.
